## About

</div>

I am a **Computer Science and Engineering student specializing in Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning**, with a particular interest in **algorithms, computer architecture, machine learning, databases, and performance-oriented systems**.

I enjoy understanding how things work beneath the abstraction — from mathematical foundations and algorithms to memory, instruction execution, and the hardware that ultimately runs the software.

My central question is simple:

> **Why does it work?**

Understanding the answer makes it possible to reason about correctness, identify unnecessary complexity, and build systems that use their resources well.

## Open Source

<img src="https://readme-typing-svg.herokuapp.com?font=Cinzel&size=17&duration=3800&pause=1400&color=D4AF37&background=00000000&center=true&vCenter=true&width=850&lines=Software+should+not+be+a+black+box.;If+there+is+a+capable+open+alternative%2C+I+want+to+know+about+it.;Millions+of+eyeballs+can+find+what+one+team+misses.;Inspect.+Verify.+Modify.+Improve." alt="Open Source Philosophy"/>

</div>

I strongly value the **open-source ecosystem**.

There is something fundamentally powerful about software whose implementation can be examined rather than merely trusted.

The scale of that collective scrutiny is one of the great strengths of open source.

> **The more eyes that can examine a system, the harder it becomes for defects to remain invisible.**

Whenever a capable open-source alternative exists, I believe it deserves consideration before automatically reaching for a proprietary solution.

## From Mathematics to Silicon

<img src="https://capsule-render.vercel.app/api?type=transparent&height=100&text=MATHEMATICS%20%E2%86%92%20ALGORITHMS%20%E2%86%92%20SOFTWARE%20%E2%86%92%20SYSTEMS%20%E2%86%92%20SILICON&fontSize=19&fontColor=D4AF37&animation=fadeIn" width="100%"/>

</div>

I am particularly interested in the layers between an abstract idea and its physical execution.

A mathematical operation becomes an algorithm.

An algorithm becomes software.

Software becomes instructions.

Instructions interact with memory, caches, pipelines, and execution units.

Ultimately, everything reaches the silicon.

Understanding those layers makes optimization more meaningful: rather than optimizing blindly, you can ask **where the actual cost comes from**.
